Ancient World
10,000 BCE &ndash; 500 BCE
Imhotep
EGYPT
c. 2650 BCE
First Engineer &amp; Architect
Designed the Step Pyramid at Saqqara &mdash; the first monumental stone structure. Physician, scribe, and advisor to Pharaoh Djoser. The original polymath before the word existed.
Relays: 1 (Fire &amp; Shelter), 4 (Stone &amp; Masonry)
&#127760; Wikipedia
"Eat, drink, and be merry, for tomorrow we shall die." &mdash; attributed
Yu the Great
CHINA
c. 2200 BCE
Flood Controller &amp; Founder of Xia Dynasty
Tamed the Yellow River through 13 years of canal-building and dredging. The first recorded infrastructure megaproject. His success founded a dynasty; his method was observation, not force.
Relays: 2 (Water &amp; Irrigation), 3 (Roads &amp; Trade)
&#127760; Wikipedia
"Rather than blocking the water, guide it." &mdash; Yu the Great
Laozi (&#x8001;&#x5B50;)
CHINA
c. 6th century BCE
Philosopher of the Way &amp; Natural Order
Author of the Tao Te Ching. His philosophy of wu wei (effortless action) and following natural patterns underpins sustainable infrastructure thinking. Water finds its own level &mdash; so should engineering.
Relays: All &mdash; The philosophical thread
&#127760; Wikipedia
"Water is the softest thing, yet it can penetrate mountains and earth." &mdash; Tao Te Ching, Ch.78
Sun Tzu (&#x5B59;&#x5B50;)
CHINA
c. 544&ndash;496 BCE
Strategist &amp; Intelligence Architect
The Art of War, Ch.13 (Use of Spies) provides the intelligence framework for IAAI's entire mobilisation model. Five types of spies = five contributor tiers. Foreknowledge wins wars and builds civilisations.
Relays: All &mdash; The strategic thread
&#127760; Wikipedia
"All warfare is based on deception... but the acme of skill is to subdue the enemy without fighting." &mdash; Art of War
Confucius (&#x5B54;&#x5B50;)
CHINA
551&ndash;479 BCE
Educator &amp; Social Architect
Established the principle that education is the foundation of civilisation. His hierarchical social model (ruler, minister, father, son) mirrors IAAI's taxonomy. Infrastructure serves society; education serves infrastructure.
Relays: All &mdash; The educational thread
&#127760; Wikipedia
"Education breeds confidence. Confidence breeds hope. Hope breeds peace." &mdash; Confucius

Classical World
500 BCE &ndash; 500 CE
Vitruvius
ROME
c. 80&ndash;15 BCE
Father of Architecture &amp; Engineering
De Architectura (Ten Books on Architecture) &mdash; the first comprehensive engineering manual. Firmitas, Utilitas, Venustas (strength, utility, beauty) remains the trinity of infrastructure design 2,000 years later.
Relays: 4 (Stone), 5 (Metals), 6 (Roads &amp; Aqueducts)
&#127760; Wikipedia
"Architecture must provide utility, strength, and beauty." &mdash; De Architectura
Archimedes
GREECE (SYRACUSE)
c. 287&ndash;212 BCE
Mathematician &amp; Mechanical Engineer
The lever, the screw, the principle of buoyancy. Proved that mathematics is the language of infrastructure. His war machines defended Syracuse; his water screw still irrigates fields today.
Relays: 2 (Water), 5 (Metals &amp; Machines)
&#127760; Wikipedia
"Give me a lever long enough and a fulcrum on which to place it, and I shall move the world." &mdash; Archimedes
Sima Qian (&#x53F8;&#x9A6C;&#x8FC1;)
CHINA
c. 145&ndash;86 BCE
Grand Historian &amp; Civilisational Recorder
Records of the Grand Historian &mdash; 130 chapters covering 2,500 years. The first systematic attempt to record civilisation's infrastructure, trade, and governance. IAAI's model of recording the relay owes everything to Sima Qian.
Relays: All &mdash; The recording thread
&#127760; Wikipedia
"A man has only one death. That death may be as heavy as Mount Tai or as light as a feather." &mdash; Sima Qian
Zhang Heng (&#x5F20;&#x8861;)
CHINA
78&ndash;139 CE
Polymath &amp; Seismograph Inventor
Invented the first seismoscope (132 CE), calculated pi to 3.1466, mapped 2,500 stars. A one-man infrastructure research lab who proved that measurement precedes management.
Relays: 5 (Metals), 7 (Measurement &amp; Science)
&#127760; Wikipedia
"The sun and moon illuminate, and the four seasons proceed in order." &mdash; Zhang Heng

MESOPOTAMIA
1136&ndash;1206
Father of Robotics &amp; Mechanical Engineering
The Book of Knowledge of Ingenious Mechanical Devices &mdash; 100 inventions including the crankshaft, camshaft, and programmable automata. The bridge between ancient water engineering and the Industrial Revolution.
Relays: 5 (Metals), 7 (Machines)
&#127760; Wikipedia
"The science of mechanics is the noblest and most useful of all sciences." &mdash; Al-Jazari
Leonardo da Vinci
ITALY
1452&ndash;1519
Universal Genius &amp; Infrastructure Visionary
Designed bridges, canals, flying machines, and urban infrastructure 400 years before they were built. His notebooks are the ultimate infrastructure sketchbook &mdash; observation, design, and imagination fused.
Relays: 6 (Bridges), 7 (Machines), 8 (Flight)
&#127760; Wikipedia
"Simplicity is the ultimate sophistication." &mdash; Leonardo da Vinci
Zheng He (&#x90D1;&#x548C;)
CHINA
1371&ndash;1433
Admiral &amp; Maritime Infrastructure Pioneer
Seven voyages with fleets of 300+ ships &mdash; the largest naval expeditions in history until the 20th century. Proved that infrastructure is not just roads and bridges; it's the networks that connect civilisations across oceans.
Relays: 3 (Trade Routes), 6 (Ships &amp; Ports)

"We have traversed more than 100,000 li of immense water spaces." &mdash; Zheng He stele

Industrial Revolution
1700 &ndash; 1900 CE
Isambard Kingdom Brunel
BRITAIN
1806&ndash;1859
Greatest Engineer of the Industrial Age
Great Western Railway, SS Great Britain, Clifton Suspension Bridge, Thames Tunnel. Built infrastructure at every scale &mdash; tunnels, bridges, ships, railways. The embodiment of the relay from horse to steam.
Relays: 6 (Ships), 7 (Loom), 8 (Rail)
&#127760; Wikipedia
"I am opposed to the laying down of rules or conditions to be observed in the construction of bridges." &mdash; Brunel
Ada Lovelace
BRITAIN
1815&ndash;1852
First Computer Programmer
Wrote the first algorithm for Babbage's Analytical Engine. Saw that computing machines could go beyond calculation to create music, art, and science. The bridge from mechanical infrastructure to digital infrastructure.
Relays: 9 (Electricity), 10 (Computing), 11 (Digital)
&#127760; Wikipedia
"The Analytical Engine weaves algebraical patterns just as the Jacquard loom weaves flowers and leaves." &mdash; Ada Lovelace
Nikola Tesla
SERBIA / USA
1856&ndash;1943
Electrical Infrastructure Pioneer
Alternating current, the induction motor, radio, and wireless power transmission. Without Tesla, there is no electrical grid, no modern infrastructure. He imagined a world powered wirelessly &mdash; we're still catching up.
Relays: 9 (Electricity), 10 (Communications)
&#127760; Wikipedia
"The present is theirs; the future, for which I really worked, is mine." &mdash; Nikola Tesla
Zhan Tianyou (&#x8A79;&#x5929;&#x4F51;)
CHINA
1861&ndash;1919
Father of Chinese Railways
Built the Beijing&ndash;Zhangjiakou Railway (1909) without foreign assistance &mdash; China's first self-designed, self-built railway. His zigzag switchback through the Badaling mountains remains an engineering masterclass.
Relays: 7 (Loom), 8 (Rail)
&#127760; Wikipedia
"Each kilometre of railway built by Chinese hands is a kilometre of national dignity." &mdash; Zhan Tianyou

BANGLADESH / USA
1929&ndash;1982
Father of Tubular Structural Design
Revolutionised skyscraper design with the tubular structure system. Willis Tower (Sears Tower) and John Hancock Center. Proved that structural innovation, not brute material, is the key to building higher.
Relays: 8 (Concrete &amp; Steel), 10 (Skyscrapers)
&#127760; Wikipedia
"The technical man must not be lost in his own technology." &mdash; Fazlur Khan
Mao Yisheng (&#x8305;&#x4EE5;&#x5347;)
CHINA
1896&ndash;1989
Bridge Engineer &amp; Educator
Designed the Qiantang River Bridge (1937) &mdash; China's first modern bridge. Then destroyed it to stop the Japanese advance, and rebuilt it after the war. Infrastructure as both creation and sacrifice for civilisation.
Relays: 6 (Bridges), 8 (Modern Infrastructure)
&#127760; Wikipedia
"A bridge is not just steel and concrete. It is the will of a people." &mdash; Mao Yisheng
Buckminster Fuller
USA

Systems Thinker &amp; Geodesic Pioneer
Geodesic domes, Dymaxion maps, "Spaceship Earth." Saw infrastructure as a whole-system problem. His question &mdash; "How much does your building weigh?" &mdash; challenged every assumption about material efficiency.
Relays: 10 (Systems), 11 (Sustainability), 12 (Torus)
&#127760; Wikipedia
"You never change things by fighting the existing reality. Build a new model that makes the existing model obsolete." &mdash; Fuller

"Any girl can be glamorous. All you have to do is stand still and look stupid. I didn't." &mdash; Hedy Lamarr
