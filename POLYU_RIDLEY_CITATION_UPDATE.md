## New Citations to Incorporate

### Primary Sources

1. **Henrich, J. (2004).** "Demography and Cultural Evolution: How Adaptive Cultural Processes Can Produce Maladaptive Losses — The Tasmanian Case." *American Antiquity*, 69(2), 197–214.
   - **Relevance:** Provides the mathematical model proving that below a critical population threshold, inter-generational knowledge transmission fails. Directly validates the Drag (D) term in AD² ≤ 16.
   - **Key finding:** Isolated populations lose technology regardless of individual cognitive capacity. Network density — not individual intelligence — determines civilisational capability.

2. **Ridley, M. (2010).** *The Rational Optimist: How Prosperity Evolves.* HarperCollins, London.
   - **Relevance:** Formalises the biological-economic thesis that civilisational progress is a function of idea exchange density ("ideas having sex"). Validates the Amplitude (A) term.
   - **Key finding:** Trade between strangers creates recombinant innovation. Isolation produces stagnation. The mechanism is identical to sexual reproduction in biology — diversity of input produces adaptive offspring.

3. **Jones, R. (1977).** "The Tasmanian Paradox." In R.V.S. Wright (ed.), *Stone Tools as Cultural Markers: Change, Evolution and Complexity*, 189–204. Australian Institute of Aboriginal Studies, Canberra.
   - **Relevance:** Original identification of the Tasmania technological regression. Coined "a slow strangulation of the mind."
   - **Key finding:** 4,000 humans with identical cognitive architecture to mainland Australians lost bone tools, fishing, hafted tools, and cold-weather clothing over 8,000 years of isolation.

4. **Henrich, J. (2016).** *The Secret of Our Success: How Culture Is Driving Human Evolution, Domesticating Our Species, and Making Us Smarter.* Princeton University Press.
   - **Relevance:** Extended thesis demonstrating that collective intelligence (cultural learning) — not individual brainpower — drives human success. Directly supports HICE as emergent network property.

5. **Kline, M.A. & Boyd, R. (2010).** "Population Size Predicts Technological Complexity in Oceania." *Proceedings of the Royal Society B*, 277(1693), 2559–2564.
   - **Relevance:** Empirical validation across 10 Oceanic island populations that larger populations maintain more complex tool kits. Confirms Relay Compression Ratio threshold.
