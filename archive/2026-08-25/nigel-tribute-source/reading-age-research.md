# Reading Age Research — 8-Year-Old (Grade 2-3)

## Key Findings from Reading Rockets / Ehri / Chall

- An 8-year-old is typically in Grade 2-3
- By end of Grade 3: typical readers have largely mastered basic word decoding skills, including multisyllabic words
- Grade 2-3 readers can decode words with common vowel patterns, consonant blends, digraphs
- They are in the "learning to read" phase (Chall's model: K-3)
- By Grade 4 they shift to "reading to learn"
- Grade 2-3 readers can handle 1-2 syllable words fluently, and are beginning to decode multisyllabic words

## Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level Reference
- Score 90-100: Grade 1 (age 6-7) — very easy, short sentences, 1-syllable words
- Score 80-90: Grade 2-3 (age 7-9) — easy, conversational English
- Score 70-80: Grade 4-5 (age 9-11) — fairly easy
- Score 60-70: Grade 6-7 (age 11-13) — plain English

## What an 8-year-old (Grade 3) can read:
- Common 1-syllable words: fire, horse, road, ship, silk, gold, trade, war, steel
- Common 2-syllable words: water, river, compass, city, empire, money, paper, powder
- Beginning to handle 3-syllable words if common: animal, adventure, discovery
- Struggles with: abstract multi-syllable words, technical jargon, Latin/Greek roots

## VERDICT
- 8 of 12 relay names are 1-syllable words (Grade 1 level, age 5-6)
- 3 of 12 are 2-syllable common words (Grade 1-3 level, age 5-8)
- 1 of 12 (COMPASS) is the most complex at 2 syllables, Grade 2-3
- NONE require more than 2 syllables
- NONE use Latin/Greek roots or technical jargon
- ALL 12 are concrete nouns — things a child can see, touch, or picture
- Average Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level for the 12 words: approximately Grade 1.5
- An 8-year-old (Grade 3) can read ALL 12 with zero difficulty
- Even a 6-year-old (Grade 1) can read 8 of 12
