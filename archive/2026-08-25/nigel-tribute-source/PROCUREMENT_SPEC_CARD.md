## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The iAAi project produces an **Engineering Discovery Toolkit** — a progressive, multi-level educational game system that teaches civil engineering through play. The product line spans 5 kit levels mapped to the iGO Lifecycle (ages 8 to 65+). All collateral is cumulative: nothing is discarded, everything builds. A Level 5 Master player holds every item from every previous level in their collection.

The core commercial thesis is simple: **85% of children do not know what civil engineering is.** They know what a "builder" is, but they do not connect that to the profession that designs cities, bridges, water systems, and the infrastructure that sustains civilisation. By the time students are 14 and choosing subjects, the recruitment window is closing. iGO is the intervention — a game that makes building visible as engineering, so 14-year-olds choose the profession that builds the world.

The product line is sold through **THE XCHANGE** (xchange.manus.space — pending build), the iAAi network's dedicated commerce platform managed by Agent 5 (ATLAS).

## 2. THE ACCUMULATION PRINCIPLE

> "You don't demolish the Roman roads to build the motorway. You build on top."

Every collateral item introduced at a lower level **remains in active use at all higher levels.** The D4 die from Level 1 is still rolled by a Level 5 Master. The starter iCards from the Explorer Kit are still referenced in the Scholar's thesis workbook. The relay map from age 8 is the same map the 40-year-old Senior Leader uses — they simply see more layers.

This principle governs all manufacturing decisions: every component must be durable enough to last a lifetime of play, and every design must accommodate the fact that it will be used alongside components from other levels.

## 6. MANUFACTURER BRIEF

### 6.1 Recommended Suppliers

The product line requires a maximum of 3-4 suppliers to cover all components:

**Primary — Game Components (Cards, Dice, Maps, Mats, Boxes):**

The Game Crafter (thegamecrafter.com) is the recommended primary supplier. They specialise in independent game production with no minimum order quantities (print-on-demand for games). They produce custom dice (all polyhedral types including D4, D8, D12, D16, D20), custom cards, game mats, fold-out maps, custom boxes, and tokens/coins. They handle fulfillment (warehouse, pick, pack, ship) and integrate with Stripe for payment processing. They are based in the USA with worldwide shipping.

For bulk orders (500+ units), Panda Game Manufacturing (pandagm.com) offers significantly lower per-unit costs but requires upfront capital investment and minimum order quantities.

**Secondary — Books & Journals:**

Lulu (lulu.com) or IngramSpark (ingramspark.com) for print-on-demand books. Both provide ISBN assignment, global distribution to Amazon/bookshops, and no minimum order quantities. Lulu is simpler for direct sales; IngramSpark has wider retail distribution.

Amazon KDP (kdp.amazon.com) as an additional channel for maximum reach, though margins are lower.

**Tertiary — Stickers & Branded Merchandise:**

StickerMule (stickermule.com) for vinyl die-cut stickers — high quality, fast turnaround, reasonable minimums (50 units).

Printful (printful.com) for any apparel or accessory merchandise (t-shirts, caps, bags) — zero inventory, print-on-demand, integrates with Stripe.

### 6.2 Quality Requirements

All components must meet the following standards, given the educational context and age range (8+):

- **Durability:** Components must withstand repeated classroom use over multiple years. Cards must be laminate-coated. Dice must be solid resin or metal, not hollow plastic.
- **Safety:** All materials must comply with EN 71 (European toy safety) and ASTM F963 (US toy safety) for ages 8+. No small parts below choking hazard size for components marketed to ages 8-10.
- **Print quality:** All cards and printed materials must be full colour, minimum 300 DPI, with consistent colour reproduction across print runs.
- **Packaging:** Each kit level must have a branded box with the iAAi identity, level number, and age recommendation clearly visible. Boxes must be sturdy enough for retail shelf display.

### 6.3 Branding Specifications

All components carry the iAAi brand identity:

- **Primary colours:** Navy #0a1628 (background), Gold #D4A843 (accent), White #FFFFFF (text)
- **Typography:** Cormorant Garamond (display), Source Sans 3 (body)

## 10. AGE & REGULATORY CONSIDERATIONS

### 10.1 Age Ratings

The iGO age bands (8-10, 10-12, etc.) are **recommended entry points**, not legal restrictions. Any age can purchase any level. The age indication on packaging follows the same convention as LEGO, board games, and educational toys — it indicates complexity, not access control.

### 10.2 Safety Compliance

For products marketed to children ages 8+, the following standards apply:

- **UK:** UK Toy Safety Regulations 2011 (SI 2011/1881), derived from EN 71
- **EU:** EN 71-1 (mechanical/physical), EN 71-2 (flammability), EN 71-3 (migration of elements)
- **US:** ASTM F963, CPSIA (Consumer Product Safety Improvement Act)
- **Marking:** CE mark (EU/UK), age warning ("Not suitable for children under 3 years" if any component is small enough)

Dice and coins must be tested for choking hazard if the kit is marketed to ages under 14. For ages 8+, standard board game components (dice, cards, tokens) are well within established safety norms.

### 10.3 Gambling Disclaimer

Because the product includes dice and is called a "game," a standard disclaimer should appear on all packaging: **"Educational game — not a game of chance. No monetary value attached to game components."** This is a precautionary measure; educational board games with dice are universally accepted and do not trigger gambling regulations.

### 10.4 Content Rating

All iGO content is educational and universal. There is no violence, adult content, or controversial material. The content covers infrastructure history, engineering principles, and civilisational development. It is suitable for all ages and all cultural contexts.
