## ADDITION 1 — Insert into Chapter 13: The 500 Generations — Relay Compression Curve

### 13A. The Ridley–Henrich Validation (Independent Biological-Economic Proof)

In 2010, Matt Ridley formalised a thesis that had been implicit in evolutionary biology since Darwin: **civilisational progress is not a function of individual intelligence but of network density of idea exchange.** His formulation — "ideas having sex" — describes the same phenomenon the Relay Compression Curve measures: when connectivity exceeds a critical threshold, capability compounds exponentially; when it falls below, capability decays regardless of individual cognitive capacity.

The mathematical proof was provided by Joseph Henrich (Harvard, 2004) through the Tasmania case study. Approximately 10,000 years ago, rising sea levels severed Tasmania from mainland Australia, isolating a population of ~4,000 humans. These humans possessed identical cognitive architecture to their mainland counterparts. Over the following 8,000 years, the Tasmanian population **lost** technologies they had previously mastered — bone tools, cold-weather clothing, fishing, hafted tools. Rhys Jones (1977) termed this "a slow strangulation of the mind."

Henrich's mathematical model demonstrated that below a critical population threshold, the probability of successful inter-generational knowledge transmission falls below replacement rate. Skills are lost faster than they can be taught. The network — not the individual — is the carrier of civilisational capability.

**The Seesaw Equation Context:**

The Seesaw Stability Framework defines equilibrium as:

> **AD² ≤ 16**

Where:
- **A** = Amplitude (network coherence, exchange density, relay connectivity)
- **D** = Drag (isolation, conflict, resource friction, cognitive overload)

When humans trade and share ideas, A rises and D falls → **stable zone** (civilisation advances).
When humans isolate, A stagnates and D rises → **collapse zone** (civilisation decays).

Tasmania is a textbook case of AD² > 16: capability decayed faster than resistance could be offset. The 4,000-person network was below the critical mass required for the Relay Compression Ratio to sustain inter-generational knowledge transfer.
