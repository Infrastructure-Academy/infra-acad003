### 44B. The Urban Scaling Proof — The Eighth Independent Validation

For more than two decades, Geoffrey West and the Santa Fe Institute have demonstrated that cities obey universal scaling laws. These laws show that as population increases, two opposing forces emerge:

- **Superlinear gains** in innovation, creativity, and economic output
- **Sublinear efficiencies** in infrastructure, energy, and resource use

This dual behaviour mirrors the Amplitude–Drag balance at the heart of the Seesaw Stability Framework.

West's core equation is:

> **Y = Y₀ × N^β**

Where:

- Y = measurable output (innovation, wages, patents, crime, energy, infrastructure)
- N = population
- β = scaling exponent

Across more than 300 cities, West found two distinct exponents:

- **Superlinear scaling (β ≈ 1.15)** — innovation, wages, patents, idea flow
- **Sublinear scaling (β ≈ 0.85)** — roads, power lines, fuel, infrastructure load

This means that as cities grow, capability compounds faster than resources, but resistance compounds faster than individuals can manage. The system remains stable only when these forces remain in equilibrium.

This is the same condition expressed by the Seesaw Stability Constant:

> **AD² ≤ 16**
